Wax Ring for Toilet Installation in Dublin, CA

A wax ring is the seal between the base of a toilet and the drain flange in the floor. If you install a new toilet or lift an old one for any reason, the ring has to be replaced, because wax that has been compressed will not seal a second time. This guide explains how to choose a ring, check the flange and spot a failing seal, written for Dublin, CA homeowners.

A leaking wax ring is slow and quiet. By the time a stain shows on the ceiling below or the floor around the toilet feels soft, water has usually been escaping for months, which is why it is worth getting the installation right.

What a wax ring does

The toilet outlet, called the horn, sits over the flange, a ring bolted to the floor on top of the drain pipe. The wax ring fills the gap between them and seals both water and sewer gas. It is compressed when the toilet is set and the closet bolts are tightened.

Standard rings are plain wax. Extra-thick rings add height for flanges that sit slightly below the floor. Rings with a plastic horn guide direct water into the drain, and rubber or foam gasket seals do the same job without wax. None of these is magic. The right one depends on the flange height.

Choosing a wax ring or seal

Match the seal to what you find at the flange, not to what the box says on the front.

What you find.Best choice.Why.
Flange flush with the finished floor.Standard wax ring.Ideal height for a standard ring..
Flange slightly below the floor.Thick wax ring or an extension.Fills the extra gap..
Flange well above the floor.Wax-free gasket or a lower ring.Avoids overcompressing the seal..
Cracked or broken flange.Repair the flange first.A new ring will not seal on a damaged flange..

Step by step: reset or install a toilet

Close the supply valve and flush the toilet to empty the tank. Disconnect the supply line, remove the nuts on the closet bolts and rock the toilet gently loose before lifting. A toilet is heavy, so get help.

Scrape off the old wax from the flange and the base. Check the flange for cracks, rust or movement, and confirm it is bolted to a solid floor. Place the new ring on the flange or on the toilet horn according to its instructions, set the toilet straight down without twisting and press it evenly.

Tighten the nuts alternately and slowly, a little on each side, until the toilet sits solidly. Over-tightening can crack the porcelain. Reconnect the supply line, open the valve and let the tank fill. Flush several times and check the base and the supply connections for drips.

Signs the ring has failed

A failing ring leaves clear signs, and the earlier you catch them the less floor damage you have to repair.

What to look for

Check these before you assume it is the tank or the supply line.

Water around the base

Seepage at the floor, especially after a flush.

A toilet that rocks

A loose toilet breaks the seal even if the ring is fine.

A musty or sewer smell

Gas escaping past a failed seal.

Stained ceiling below

A toilet on an upper floor leaking through the subfloor.

When it is a flange problem

Flanges can crack, corrode or sit too low after a floor change. If new tile or vinyl was laid over the old floor, the flange may be below the surface, and a standard ring will not reach. Metal flanges rust, and plastic ones can split.

A repair ring or an extension can restore the flange. If the subfloor around it is soft, the subfloor needs repair first. That is the point where a plumber is worth calling, because a repair that misses a soft floor will fail again. See our toilet repair and installation page for how we handle it.

Choosing and installing a new toilet

Measure the rough-in, the distance from the finished wall to the flange center. Twelve inches is the most common, but ten and fourteen exist, and a toilet made for a different rough-in will not fit. Check the clearance to side walls too, about fifteen inches from the center is a common guide.

Comfort-height and elongated bowls suit many adults. Dual-flush models save water. Mounting the toilet on an old, scaled flange is the most common reason a new toilet leaks. If the toilet has a recurring clog, a camera check of the line may be a better investment than a new bowl, and our drain cleaning page explains that.

Dublin, CA: permits and fixtures

The City of Dublin publishes a handout of work that is exempt from permit. It lists clearing stoppages or repairing leaks in pipes, valves, fittings or fixtures as exempt, as long as the repair does not involve replacing or rearranging them, and it says replacing or rearranging pipes, valves, fittings or fixtures is not exempt. Resetting a toilet with a new wax ring is a repair, while moving a toilet to a new location is not.

If you are unsure whether your toilet job is exempt, ask the City of Dublin Building and Safety Division before starting. Source type for this note: a City of Dublin permit handout.

Questions about this topic

Can I reuse a wax ring?

No. Wax that has been compressed will not reseal reliably. Use a new ring every time the toilet is lifted.

How long does a wax ring last?

A properly installed ring can last for years. A ring fails sooner if the toilet rocks, if the flange is damaged or if the floor shifts.

Is a wax-free gasket better?

It is easier to install and reusable in some cases, and it suits flanges that are slightly above or below the floor. Wax rings are inexpensive and work well on a flange at the right height.

Do I need a permit to replace a toilet in Dublin, CA?

The City of Dublin exempts repairs to fixtures that do not involve replacing or rearranging them, and it does not exempt replacing or rearranging fixtures. Ask the Building and Safety Division about your specific job.
