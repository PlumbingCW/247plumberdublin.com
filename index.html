Dublin, California.

Plumbing in a city that grew fast

Dublin incorporated on February 1, 1982. The City of Dublin says it had nearly 14,000 residents in 1981 and more than 65,000 in 2020. That growth built neighborhoods around Emerald Glen Park, Don Biddle Community Park and Fallon Sports Park, along Dublin Boulevard and beside the BART stations, and it means pipes from several building eras sit under the same city.

Water here is delivered by the Dublin San Ramon Services District, which buys wholesale water from Zone 7 Water Agency. Zone 7 reports that its groundwater is above 300 mg/L in hardness and its surface water below 100 mg/L, and that supply is typically about 80 percent surface water and 20 percent groundwater.

Hardness shapes how fast scale builds in heaters, faucets and tankless units, which is why our water softener and filtration and tankless water heater pages talk about it.

Dispatch works from Dublin Boulevard and reaches the Camp Parks area, the streets near the Iron Horse Regional Trail and the neighborhoods along I-580 and I-680. Nearby towns are covered too, and you can read about each on its own page.

Plumbing here follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) from IAPMO, and the plumber explains whether the City of Dublin requires a permit for your job.

Common questions.

Questions we hear in Dublin

Is your plumbing dispatch really open all night?

Yes. Dispatch answers every hour of every day, including weekends and holidays. Call (510) 544-1962 and a person will take down what is happening and arrange a plumber.

What should I do before the plumber arrives?

Shut off the water at the main valve if water is spreading, turn off the water heater if it is leaking, and move belongings away from the wet area. Do not run drains that are backing up.

Do you serve homes and businesses in Dublin, CA?

Yes. We take calls from houses, townhomes, apartments, restaurants, shops and offices across Dublin, and from Pleasanton, San Ramon, Livermore and Danville.

Will I know the price before work starts?

Yes. The plumber explains the problem and the cost before starting, so you can decide. If you want a sense of cost first, call and describe the job.

Which plumbing problems count as emergencies?

Burst pipes, active leaks, sewer backups, no water, gas-fired water heater trouble and a water heater leaking onto the floor all qualify. If you are unsure, call and ask.
