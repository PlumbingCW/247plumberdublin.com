Start with your water bill: two utilities in one city

San Ramon has a feature that changes how we diagnose a plumbing problem: more than one agency supplies its water. The Dublin San Ramon Services District serves the Dougherty Valley portion of the city, the same agency that serves Dublin, while another utility serves other parts of the city, and the name on your bill tells you which. That matters when a leak is on the meter side, because the agency that owns the meter is the one to call.

For a homeowner on the Dougherty Valley side, the water arrives through the Dublin San Ramon Services District, which buys it from Zone 7 Water Agency. That is the same wholesale source as Dublin. On the other side, the supply comes through a different retail agency with its own pressure and rules.

We ask for the utility name at the start of a call, and we mention it in our notes when the issue is a high bill, low pressure or a meter that keeps running. Our leak detection and water service line repair pages describe how we work through each.

If a pipe bursts or water is spreading in San Ramon

Start at the shutoff. San Ramon homes may have the main valve on the exterior wall near the garage, at a side yard box or in a utility closet. Close it fully, then open a faucet at the lowest point of the house to drain the lines.

Next, protect what you can. Move rugs, electronics and paper out of the wet area and switch off breakers for any room with standing water. We continue by phone with the shutoff steps while a plumber is dispatched from Dublin.

Newer homes, different problems

Much of San Ramon grew after its 1983 incorporation, so a large share of its housing dates from the 1980s onward. That means PEX and copper supply lines, plastic drain pipe and water heaters that are a decade or more into their life. Failures here are more often a worn water heater, a supply line, a faucet cartridge or a slab-on-grade leak than a corroded galvanized pipe.

If the house has a slab, a warm floor patch is a warning sign, and our slab leak repair page covers the process. Drain problems usually involve grease or tree roots in a lateral rather than old cast iron, and drain cleaning with a camera pass shows the difference.

For a home built in the late 1980s or early 1990s, we also check for polybutylene supply lines, which were used widely in that window and are known to fail at fittings.

Codes and permits in San Ramon

Plumbing work in California follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) from IAPMO. Permit rules are set by each city, so a job outside Dublin follows the local building department, and our plumber tells you before work begins if a permit applies.
