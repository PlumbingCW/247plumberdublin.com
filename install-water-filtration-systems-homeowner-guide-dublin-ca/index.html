How to Install a Water Filtration System: A Dublin, CA Homeowner Guide

Installing a water filtration system starts with a question: what do you want removed? A filter that improves taste does not remove hardness, and one that traps sediment does not remove chlorine. For Dublin, CA homeowners, the answer begins with the water quality report your supplier publishes and a few simple tests at your own tap.

This guide walks through the types of filters, where they install and what a plumber does that a homeowner usually cannot.

What filters do and do not do

A sediment filter traps particles like sand and rust. A carbon filter reduces chlorine taste and odor, and some organic compounds. Reverse osmosis forces water through a membrane to remove dissolved solids, usually at a single tap. None of these softens water unless it is built to, so scale is a separate problem.

Be clear about the goal. If your water tastes of chlorine, carbon is the answer. If it is cloudy, sediment is. If you want to reduce dissolved minerals in drinking water, reverse osmosis fits.

Common filter types

Match the filter to the problem.

Installation basics

A whole-house filter housing mounts on the main line with shutoff valves on both sides and a pressure gauge if you want to track filter loading. A plumber cuts the pipe, installs unions or flexible connectors and tests for leaks. An under-sink unit connects to a cold supply with a saddle valve or a tee, and reverse osmosis units also need a drain connection and a storage tank.

Mount filters where the cartridges can be changed. A housing in a tight spot will go unmaintained. Keep a spare cartridge and a housing wrench on hand.

Maintenance that matters

A filter is only as good as its upkeep.

Change cartridges on schedule

A clogged filter cuts flow and can grow bacteria.

Watch the gauge

A pressure drop across the filter shows loading.

Flush after changing

Run water for a few minutes.

Sanitize reverse osmosis yearly

Follow the manufacturer instructions.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistakes are buying a system before knowing the problem, mounting a housing where it cannot be reached and ignoring cartridge changes. Another is installing a whole-house filter on a line with weak pressure, which makes showers worse.

A short conversation with a plumber before you buy usually prevents all of these. Bring photos of the space and your test results.

Testing before you buy

A water test tells you what to treat. A basic home test strip kit gives rough numbers for hardness, chlorine and pH. A lab test gives more detail and is worth the cost if you suspect a specific contaminant. Avoid sales events that test a cup of water at your table, because the test is part of the pitch.

Bring your results to a plumber and describe what bothers you, whether it is taste, smell, spots on glasses or scale on fixtures. The right system is the one that solves your problem with the least equipment, and sometimes that is a single under-sink filter.

Cost to own, not just to buy

The purchase price is only part of what a filter system costs. Cartridges, membranes, salt and power add up over the years. Ask for the replacement schedule and the price of cartridges before you commit, and check whether you can buy them locally.

A system that is cheap to buy but hard to maintain tends to be neglected, and a neglected filter does more harm than none. Pick equipment you will actually service.

Where plumbing meets filtration

Filters add resistance, so a whole-house system on a home with low pressure can leave showers weak. A pressure test before you buy tells you how much margin you have. If you have galvanized pipe inside the house, a filter will not fix the rust that comes from it. Repiping does, and our repiping page explains that.

For a softener or a combined system, see water softener and filtration. We also install fixtures and under-sink connections as part of plumbing installation.

Dublin, CA: read your water quality report first

Zone 7 Water Agency, the wholesaler that supplies the Dublin San Ramon Services District, publishes annual water quality reports. The reports list what is in the water it delivers and the hardness figures it gives in mg/L. Start there, and ask the district for the report for your address, then decide which problem you want a filter to solve.

Zone 7 also says water at the tap is typically about 80 percent surface water and 20 percent groundwater. Source type: Zone 7 Water Agency consumer confidence reports and its hard water FAQ.

Questions about this topic

Do I need a whole-house filter or an under-sink filter?

Under-sink filters handle drinking water at one tap. A whole-house filter treats everything. Choose by your goal.

Will a filter soften water?

Not unless it is designed to. A softener or conditioner does that.

How often should I change filter cartridges?

Follow the manufacturer schedule. A pressure gauge can also show when a cartridge is loaded.

Where do I find the water quality report for Dublin, CA?

Ask the Dublin San Ramon Services District, and look at Zone 7 Water Agency, which publishes annual water quality reports.
