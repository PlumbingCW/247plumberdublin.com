How to Install an Instant Hot Water Heater Under the Sink in Dublin, CA

An instant hot water heater under the sink is a small unit that supplies hot water to one faucet without waiting for the main water heater. In a Dublin, CA home with a long pipe run to the kitchen, it can remove the wait and the wasted water. It is also a project with electrical, plumbing and space requirements that are worth checking first.

This guide covers what to measure, how installation works and when another approach fits better.

Types of point-of-use hot water units

Mini tank heaters store a few gallons and hold it hot, so water comes out right away. Tankless point-of-use units heat water as it flows and need more electrical power. Instant hot dispensers, which deliver near-boiling water from a separate tap, are another category.

Each one needs space under the sink, a cold water supply, an electrical outlet and sometimes a dedicated circuit.

Installation steps

Measure the cabinet, the clearance for the unit and the distance to the nearest outlet. Check the electrical load. A mini tank usually plugs into a standard outlet, and a tankless unit may need a dedicated circuit that an electrician should install.

Shut off the water at the angle stop, install a tee on the hot supply or a dedicated line, mount the heater, connect the pressure relief where required and check for leaks. Set the thermostat to a safe temperature to avoid scalding, and test before closing the cabinet.

Things that go wrong

Common issues with under-sink units.

Not enough room

The cabinet is too small for the unit.

Wrong electrical supply

An overloaded circuit or an unsuitable outlet.

Scale

Hard water builds up in small heating elements.

Leaks at connections

Loose fittings or old shutoff valves.

Maintenance for a small heater

Small units need less care than a main heater, but they are not maintenance free. Check the connections for drips every few months, flush a mini tank once a year if the manufacturer allows it, and test the relief valve according to the manual. In hard water, descaling keeps the element efficient and extends its life.

If the unit starts to take longer to heat, trips a breaker or leaves mineral flakes in the aerator, call for service rather than waiting. Small heaters are inexpensive to replace, but a leak in a cabinet can ruin the floor of the cabinet quickly.

Cost and savings in plain terms

A point-of-use unit has a purchase cost, an installation cost and a running cost. The running cost comes from electricity to keep a small tank warm or to heat water on demand. Savings come from water you no longer pour down the drain while waiting, and from the convenience of immediate hot water.

If your home has a long run from the main heater to the kitchen, the wait is long and the saving is real. If the heater is close to the sink, the benefit is small and a point-of-use unit may not be worth it. A plumber can measure the run and tell you which case you are in.

Safety checks before you finish

Set the thermostat to a temperature that will not scald, especially in homes with children or older adults. Confirm that the pressure relief device on a mini tank has a safe discharge path. Test the shutoff valve so you can isolate the unit later, and check that the power cord is not touching the supply lines.

Finally, label the circuit breaker and keep the manual with the other home records. When the unit needs service in a few years, you will be glad to know the model and the breaker.

Related fixes

If the problem is a slow wait at every tap, a recirculation pump on the main heater may help more. If the kitchen tap runs weak, a clogged aerator or a failing valve may be the cause, and our faucet repair page covers that.

Installing a new unit may involve moving or adding plumbing, which is part of plumbing installation. Also consider the age and size of your main heater first.

Dublin, CA: hardness and permits

Zone 7 Water Agency, which supplies the Dublin San Ramon Services District, says its groundwater is over 300 mg/L in hardness and surface water under 100 mg/L. Small heating elements scale up quickly in hard water, so flushing or descaling matters more for a small point-of-use unit.

Permit rules vary by project. The City of Dublin's handout says replacing or rearranging pipes, valves, fittings or fixtures is not exempt, so ask the Building and Safety Division. Source type: Zone 7 hard water FAQ and the City of Dublin permit handout.

Questions about this topic

Do I need an electrician?

For a tankless point-of-use unit, a dedicated circuit is often needed. Mini tank units may use a standard outlet. Check the unit rating.

Will it save water?

It saves the water that runs down the drain while you wait for hot water.

Is it safe to install under a sink?

Yes, when installed to the manufacturer instructions with clearances, a relief valve where required and a safe thermostat setting.

Does Dublin water scale small heaters?

Hardness can vary by source. Test your tap and plan for descaling.
