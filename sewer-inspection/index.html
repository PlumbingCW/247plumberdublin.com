Sewer camera inspection in Dublin, CA means feeding a waterproof camera on a flexible cable into your sewer line and watching live video of the pipe wall. The footage shows root intrusion, cracks, offset joints, sags that hold water and grease buildup, and a locator tells us how deep the line is and where it runs.

It is the most useful step after a repeated backup and before buying a home with an older sewer line. A camera takes the guesswork out of whether the line needs cleaning, lining or replacing.

What the camera shows

The camera head has lights and a wide-angle lens and is pushed through a cleanout. A cable counter records the distance from the entry point, so any defect is tied to a footage mark. A sonde in the camera head emits a signal that a hand-held locator picks up on the surface, so we know the depth and the position of the line.

We record the run and give you the findings in plain language. Common findings include roots entering at joints, hairline or longitudinal cracks, offset joints where one section has shifted, bellies (low spots that hold water), grease and scale buildup, and sometimes a collapsed section.

Reasons to inspect

These are the situations where we recommend a camera.

Repeated backups

If cabling keeps working only temporarily, something in the line is causing it.

Buying or selling a home

A sewer scope shows a defect that a general inspection cannot see.

Before landscaping or hardscape

Know where the line runs before digging or planting.

After a cleaning

A post-cleaning run shows the actual condition of the pipe wall.

What each finding usually means

Findings are not all equal. A single hairline crack is different from a collapsed pipe.

Finding.What it means.Common next step.
Light root intrusion at a joint.Joint is open, roots feed on the water.Root cutting, monitor, or point repair.
Offset joint.Pipe sections have shifted.Spot repair if short, liner if continuous.
Belly or sag.Low spot holds water and debris.Repair or replacement if it keeps clogging.
Heavy grease and scale.Pipe wall is coated.Hydro jetting.
Collapse.Pipe has failed.Excavation or pipe bursting.

Home buyers and sellers

A sewer scope is a standard step in many home purchases. The camera can find a problem that costs thousands to fix, and the video gives you leverage in negotiating. For sellers, an inspection before listing lets you address a problem on your terms.

We give you a recorded video and a plain written description of what we saw. We do not tell you what to buy or sell, and we do not act as a home inspector. We describe the pipe and the options.

The Dublin sewer system

The Dublin San Ramon Services District provides wastewater collection and treatment for Dublin and San Ramon. The property owner is generally responsible for the lateral from the house to the connection with the district main. If the camera shows a defect on the district side, we tell you and help you contact the district.

Our camera work is available across Dublin, including neighborhoods around Emerald Glen Park, Don Biddle Community Park and the older streets close to Dublin Boulevard, and in Pleasanton and San Ramon.

Codes and permits

Plumbing in California follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) published by IAPMO. Our work on sewer camera inspection is planned with that code in mind, and the City of Dublin decides when a permit is needed.

Questions we hear in Dublin

How long does a sewer camera inspection take?

Typically an hour or two, depending on the length of the line and whether it needs cleaning first.

Do I get a copy of the video?

Yes. We give you the recording and a plain description of the findings.

Can the camera find the cause of a backup?

Often yes. It shows roots, grease, cracks, offsets and sags that explain repeated backups.

Does every home sale need a sewer scope?

No, but many buyers choose one, especially for older homes. It is optional and priced separately from a general inspection.
