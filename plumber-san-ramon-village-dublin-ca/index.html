Where San Ramon Village is

San Ramon Village is a residential neighborhood in Dublin, California, ZIP code 94568. The name confuses people because the City of San Ramon is a separate city to the north. A 2025 history on the TractTales blog places the original tract between Alcosta Boulevard and Amador Valley Road, with a later unit north of the original streets and east of I-680.

The City of Dublin lists Kolb Park as sitting between Brighton Drive and Bristol Road, and a real estate guide calls the park the heart of San Ramon Village. We use those streets as anchors. No public source we found draws a precise boundary, so an address near an edge should be treated as approximate.

What is documented about the homes

The TractTales history says Volk-McLain Communities built the tract, that the grand opening was in September 1960 and that a fifth unit was announced in February 1961. Advertised floor plans ran from about 1,038 to 1,475 square feet, with three or four bedrooms and two bathrooms, in Cape Cod, Colonial, Ranch and Contemporary exteriors.

A real estate guide describes most single-family homes here as ranch style on small lots and gives a median year built of 1963. Those figures describe the original tract. They say nothing about the inside of any one house today, because sixty years of owners have repaired, remodeled and added to these homes.

General plumbing questions for a tract this age

The next points are general plumbing information, not findings about particular streets. A house built around 1960 is now more than 60 years old, so its drains, supply lines and water heater can be in very different condition depending on what earlier owners replaced. Looking is the only reliable way to know.

For a slow main drain or repeated backups, a sewer camera inspection shows the pipe from the cleanout to the connection. That lets you choose between drain cleaning and sewer line repair with evidence in hand. Roots, shifting soil and aged joints are the usual general causes, and the camera shows which one applies.

If several fixtures leak, run discolored or lose pressure, ask the plumber to identify the pipe material before anyone quotes a repipe. A tank heater that keeps needing repairs is a candidate for replacement, and a plumber can tell you how old yours is from the label.

What we could not verify

We found no public source that lists the pipe materials, sewer lateral condition or past plumbing repairs in this neighborhood, so this page makes no claim about them. The sourcing for the tract history is a hobbyist blog that cites period newspaper ads, and the City of Dublin does not publish a boundary map for the name.

Codes and permits in Dublin

Plumbing work here follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) from IAPMO. The City of Dublin decides when a permit is needed, so ask before a larger job.

Questions from San Ramon Village homeowners

Is San Ramon Village part of the City of San Ramon?

No. Public sources place it in Dublin, ZIP code 94568. The City of San Ramon is a separate city, and we cover it on its own page: emergency plumber San Ramon.

Can you tell what pipes my house has from the neighborhood?

No. Tract records describe the original homes, not what later owners changed. A plumber identifies the material at the meter, the water heater and under the sinks.

Does a drain repair in Dublin need a permit?

The City of Dublin has a handout of permit-exempt work that includes clearing a stoppage and repairing a leak. Replacing fixtures or rearranging pipes is not on that list, so ask the city before a larger job.
