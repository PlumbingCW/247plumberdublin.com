Plumber in Silvergate, Dublin, CA

Silvergate is a 1960s ranch-home subdivision in the hills of west Dublin. This page covers what public sources document about it and why hillside homes raise their own plumbing questions.

Where Silvergate is

Silvergate is a subdivision in west Dublin, in the hills just west of San Ramon Village. A 2025 TractTales history says developer Tom Gentry bought 250 acres of hilly farmland there. It names San Sabana Court, Castilian Road and Silvergate Drive as the streets where the model homes stood, and we use those as anchors.

The edges are fuzzy. A separate TractTales history of the neighboring Briarhill tract also names Silvergate Drive, so streets and names overlap. If your address is near Hansen Drive or the hillside above the tract, the neighborhood name may not match what the county calls it.

What is documented about the homes

The same history says the neighborhood was built between 1962 and 1968 and describes 400 ranch-style homes with open floor plans, two-car garages and large backyards, with some in-ground pools. Listed models ranged from about 1,348 to 2,035 square feet. Advertised prices in 1962 started at $18,800.

We could not confirm the 400-home count from a second source, so treat it as the author's figure. A real estate guide names Silvergate among Dublin neighborhoods but gives no year built or boundary.

Why hillside homes have their own plumbing questions

Treat the next points as general plumbing information, not a survey of this neighborhood. Water pressure falls by about 0.43 psi for every foot you climb above the supply main, so a house 100 feet higher than its neighbors gets roughly 43 psi less to work with. Homes at different elevations on the same street can have very different pressure.

Low pressure and high pressure both cause trouble. Weak pressure shows up as slow showers and failing washing machines, while very high pressure strains fixtures and connections and can make a hidden leak worse. A plumber can read static pressure with a gauge on a hose bib and tell you whether the number is reasonable.

A steady leak is not always visible in a yard with a pool, an irrigation system and a long run from the street. Leak detection with a meter test and listening gear finds it without digging at random.

A break between the meter and the house is a water service line repair. If a pipe has split inside a wall, burst pipe repair starts with the main shutoff, and a heater that stops delivering hot water is a water heater repair question.

What we could not verify

No public source we found gives pressure readings, pipe materials or past repairs for Silvergate streets, so this page claims none. The pressure figure above is physics, not a measurement of any one house, and the pool detail comes from the builder history, not from a survey.

Nearby areas and related pages

Codes and permits in Dublin

The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), applies to work in this neighborhood. The City of Dublin treats clearing a stoppage and repairing a leak differently from replacing fixtures or moving pipes, so ask which applies to you.

Questions from Silvergate homeowners

Where is Silvergate in Dublin?

Public sources place it in the hills of west Dublin, just west of San Ramon Village, around Silvergate Drive, Castilian Road and San Sabana Court. See the San Ramon Village page for the neighboring tract.

Why is my water pressure low or high?

Elevation, the supply main and any pressure regulator all play a part. A gauge reading at a hose bib tells a plumber more than guessing, and you can ask for that reading when you call.

How do I find a leak under a yard or pool deck?

Start by checking whether the meter dial moves with every fixture off. If it does, a plumber can narrow the leak to the line, a fixture or the irrigation with listening and pressure tests.
