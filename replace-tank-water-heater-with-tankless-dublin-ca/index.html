Replace a Tank Water Heater with Tankless in Dublin, CA

Replacing a tank water heater with a tankless model is more than a swap. A tankless unit heats water on demand, which saves space and avoids the cold shower at the end of the line, but it asks more of the gas line, the venting and the maintenance. For Dublin, CA homeowners, a few checks before the purchase decide whether it makes sense.

This guide lists those checks, the typical differences and what to expect during the installation.

What changes when you go tankless

A tank heater has a burner of modest size that runs for a while. A tankless heater has a large burner that fires only when water flows. That burner often draws far more gas than the old tank, so the gas line may be too small. The venting is usually different too, and many models need a stainless or plastic vent and a condensate drain.

The wall space matters. Tankless units mount on a wall, and the location needs clearances and access for service.

Cost and payback

A tankless installation costs more than a like-for-like tank swap. Savings come from lower standby heat loss, since there is no tank to keep hot, and from a longer expected life. Payback depends on how much hot water you use and what the gas line and venting work cost.

For a small household that uses little hot water, the payback may be long. For a larger household, the benefit grows. We say so honestly. If your tank has failed, see water heater replacement for the same-day option.

What happens to the old tank

A removed tank heater is heavy and contains sediment and water, so the plumber drains it and takes it away. Ask that disposal be included in the quote. Metal tanks are generally recyclable, and a reputable installer handles that for you.

Keep the paperwork for the old unit if you plan to claim any incentive, because some programs ask for proof of replacement. Take a photo of the label before the unit leaves your property.

What the installation day looks like

A tank-to-tankless conversion takes longer than a like-for-like swap. The plumber drains and removes the old tank, mounts the new unit, runs the new vent, connects the water and gas lines and installs isolation valves for future flushing. If the gas line needs upsizing, that adds work, and an electrician may be needed for the outlet.

Plan to be without hot water for much of the day. Ask the plumber to walk you through the controls, the temperature settings and the error codes before leaving, and keep the manual where you can find it.

Questions to ask before you commit

Ask the plumber to measure your gas line and confirm the unit is sized for your highest demand, such as a shower and a dishwasher running together. Ask whether the vent will be sealed and where it will exit. Ask what the maintenance schedule looks like in your water and who will do it.

A good answer is specific. If the response is vague about the gas line or the vent, ask again. These two items are where most surprise costs in a tankless conversion come from.

Maintenance and descaling

Tankless units need annual flushing in hard water, an inlet filter check and a look at the vent. Isolation valves make flushing simple, so ask for them at installation. Our tankless water heater page covers flush and repair.

If your water is hard, consider treating it. Scale in the heat exchanger shortens the unit's life.

Dublin, CA: permits and water hardness

Permit rules vary by project, so ask the City of Dublin Building and Safety Division. Changing a heater type, adding a gas line or moving the vent are the kinds of work that can need a permit.

Zone 7 Water Agency, the wholesaler for the Dublin San Ramon Services District, says its groundwater is over 300 mg/L in hardness and its surface water is under 100 mg/L, with a typical 80 percent surface mix. Test your tap to decide how often to flush. Source type: Zone 7 hard water FAQ and the City permit page.
