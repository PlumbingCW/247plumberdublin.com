Tankless water heater service in Dublin, CA includes installing a new unit, repairing a unit that has stopped heating and flushing scale out of one that is slowing down. A tankless heater heats water as it flows instead of storing it, so it saves space and does not run out during a long shower, but it has its own demands on gas, venting and maintenance.

If your unit shows an error code, has stopped producing hot water or fluctuates in temperature, call and tell us the model and the code. Many faults come from scale in the heat exchanger, a clogged inlet screen or a venting problem.

Tankless installation: what changes from a tank

A tankless heater needs a gas line large enough to feed a high burner rating, and many older gas lines were sized for a tank. It also needs specific venting, often a sealed system in stainless or plastic, and a condensate drain on condensing models. A water heater swap that fits a tank in a closet does not always fit a tankless unit.

We check gas line size, vent routing and clearance before quoting. We also look at the water supply and recommend isolation valves so the unit can be flushed. Isolation valves are inexpensive and are the single most useful addition to any tankless install.

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Dublin Building and Safety Division. We describe the work in the terms they use.

Tankless flush and descaling

Scale builds in the heat exchanger and narrows the water passages. The unit then works harder, produces less hot water, and eventually throws an error code. A flush circulates a descaling solution, usually white vinegar or a manufacturer-approved product, through the heat exchanger for an hour or so, then rinses it out.

Dublin water is a blend that can include harder groundwater. Zone 7 Water Agency reports groundwater above 300 mg/L in hardness and surface water below 100 mg/L, and the district that serves Dublin buys from Zone 7. More scale means more frequent flushing, and a conditioner or softener upstream can reduce it. Manufacturer manuals typically recommend an annual flush, and more often in hard water.

Common tankless problems

These are the issues we see most often.

Cold water sandwich

Hot water, a brief cold burst, then hot again. Often a flow, scale or thermostat issue.

Error codes

Ignition, flame sensor, venting or flow sensor faults.

Low flow

A clogged inlet filter or scaled heat exchanger.

No hot water

A gas supply, ignition or power problem.

Repairing a tankless water heater

We start with the display. Error codes narrow the fault to ignition, flame rod, exhaust, water flow or thermal sensors. Common repairs include cleaning the flame sensor, replacing an inlet screen, replacing a thermistor and clearing a blocked vent.

Not all tankless faults are repairable economically. A cracked heat exchanger on an older unit may cost more to replace than the unit is worth. We tell you honestly and give the numbers.

What to do if a tankless heater fails

Most problems are inconvenient, but a gas smell is not.

Gas smell

Leave the building, do not touch switches, and call 911 or your gas utility from outside.

Error code

Note the code and turn the unit off and on once. If it returns, call us.

Water leak

Close the unit's water valves and call. Turn off power to the unit if you can do so safely.

No hot water

Check that the gas valve is open and that a breaker has not tripped.

Questions we hear in Dublin

How often should a tankless heater be flushed?

Manufacturers commonly recommend annual flushing, and more often in hard water. Check your manual and your water.

Do I need a bigger gas line for tankless?

Often yes. Many tankless units draw much more gas than a tank. We check before quoting.

Why do I get a burst of cold water?

It can be a flow rate issue, scale or a sensor fault. We diagnose the cause.

Is tankless worth it?

It depends on space, hot water demand and installation conditions. It is a good fit for some homes and not for others.
