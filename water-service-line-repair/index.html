Written by the Dublin 24/7 Plumbing team. Updated November 16, 2026.

Emergency water service line repair in Dublin, CA is about the underground pipe between the water meter and your house. A leak there can soak a yard, drop your pressure and run up the bill, and because the pipe is buried, the first call should be to a plumber who can locate the break before anyone digs.

If water is surfacing in the yard or the meter runs with everything off, close the house main valve and call. If the leak is before the house valve, you may need the district shutoff at the meter, and we can tell you whether you may operate it.

Water line break emergency: what to do first

A broken service line can release a lot of water in a short time. Shut the house main if it still holds. If water keeps coming from the ground, the leak is upstream of the valve, which means the service line itself.

Dispatch can coach you through the shutoff and send a crew to locate the break. We also advise on notifying the district if the problem is near the meter, since the meter belongs to the district.

Symptoms that point to the service line

Not all of these mean the same thing, so we confirm before digging.

Soggy patch or sinkhole in the yard

Water is surfacing along the pipe path.

Sudden pressure loss

A break or a partly closed valve restricts flow.

Meter runs with the house off

Water is escaping between the meter and the house.

Brown water after a repair

Sediment stirred up by changes in flow.

Repair, replace or reroute

A single leak on a sound line can be repaired with a short section. If the line is galvanized, old copper or polybutylene, repeated leaks are likely, and replacement may be cheaper over time. We explain the age, material and number of repairs so you can decide.

Replacement can be done in a trench or with a trenchless method such as pipe bursting, which pulls a new pipe through the old path with small entry pits. Trenchless saves lawns and driveways but needs the right conditions. We check the line before recommending one.

The district owns the meter and the pipe up to it, and the pipe from the meter to the house is generally the owner's responsibility. Ask the Dublin San Ramon Services District where its responsibility ends for your address.

Low water pressure

Low pressure has many causes: a partly closed valve, a failing regulator, mineral buildup in old galvanized pipe, a leak, or a problem on the district side. Check a pressure gauge on a hose bib. Normal residential pressure is roughly 40 to 80 psi.

If pressure is low at every fixture, the problem is probably upstream of the house plumbing. If only one fixture is weak, the cause is local to it. We test the whole system and tell you where the restriction is.

Dublin: DSRSD, Zone 7 and underground work

Water in Dublin comes from the Dublin San Ramon Services District, which buys from Zone 7 Water Agency. The district serves Dublin and the Dougherty Valley part of San Ramon. Water pressure and service-line conditions vary by neighborhood, and the age of the house gives a rough guide to the material.

Any dig needs an 811 call for underground utility marking, and a project may need a permit. Ask the City of Dublin Building and Safety Division. We also service lines in Livermore and Danville.

Codes and permits

The California Plumbing Code is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and it sets the baseline for water service line repair in Dublin. Clearing a stoppage or repairing a leak is on the City of Dublin permit-exempt list, while replacing fixtures or rearranging pipes is not, so ask us whether your job needs one.

Questions we hear in Dublin

Do you handle water line emergencies at night or on weekends in Dublin?

Yes. Dispatch is available 24/7. Shut the house main and call. We start the locating and plan the repair.

Who owns the water line from the meter to my house?

Generally the property owner. The district owns the meter. Confirm with the Dublin San Ramon Services District for your address.

Can the line be replaced without trenching the whole yard?

Often, yes, with trenchless methods. It depends on the path and the soil. We check before recommending.

Why did my water pressure suddenly drop?

A break, a failing regulator, a partly closed valve or a problem upstream. We test pressure at the hose bib and the meter to find where it is lost.
