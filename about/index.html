Written by the Dublin 24/7 Plumbing team. Updated November 16, 2026.

Dublin 24/7 Plumbing exists for the moment a plumbing problem will not wait for business hours. We run a dispatch line that answers at any hour, takes down what is happening and sends a licensed, insured plumber.

Most of our calls are the ones people dread: water on the floor, a drain that has backed up, a heater that has stopped or a pipe that has split. We keep the process simple. You call, we ask what you see, we tell you how to limit the damage and the plumber explains the repair and the cost before starting.

What a call looks like

When you call, dispatch asks where you are, what you see and whether water is still running. If it is, you are walked to the main shutoff, which is usually near the water meter or where the line enters the house, and told how to turn it off. If a water heater is leaking, you are told to turn off its power or gas and its cold supply valve.

Then a plumber is assigned and given what you described, so the first minutes on site go to the fix instead of to questions. If the problem looks bigger than first described, the plumber tells you before work begins and explains the new options.

For problems that are not urgent, such as a dripping faucet or a slow drain, dispatch books a visit and tells you what to expect. You do not need to wait for an emergency to call.

How we work

We do not publish a flat price list, because the same symptom can have very different causes. A slow drain may be a hair clog or a root-filled sewer line. The plumber finds the cause, tells you the options and gives you the price before the work begins.

We also explain when a repair is not worth it. A water heater that leaks from the tank is a replacement, and we say so rather than sell a repair that will not hold.

Why Dublin

Dublin is a young city. The City of Dublin says it incorporated on February 1, 1982, and that its population grew from nearly 14,000 in 1981 to more than 65,000 in 2020. That growth shapes the work. A home near Dublin Boulevard may have plumbing from the 1970s, while a house near Emerald Glen Park may be much newer, and a call for one is a different job than for the other.

The Dublin San Ramon Services District delivers water here, and it buys wholesale water from Zone 7 Water Agency. Zone 7 reports that its groundwater is above 300 mg/L in hardness and its surface water is below 100 mg/L. Hardness affects water heaters and fixtures, so we talk about it on the pages where it matters.

Where we work

Our base is in Dublin, and we dispatch across the city and to Pleasanton, San Ramon, Livermore and Danville. Each town has a page with local details, starting with Pleasanton and San Ramon.

Our service pages explain each type of job, what causes it and what affects the cost, and our blog goes deeper on topics like hard water, water heaters and leak detection. Our work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Planned work and second opinions

Not every call is an emergency. If you are planning a water heater replacement, a repipe or a softener, call during the day and ask for a visit. A plumber looks at the space, the pipe material and the water pressure, then explains the options in plain terms.

We also give second opinions. If another company told you a pipe needs full replacement, we will look and tell you whether a repair would hold, and why.

What we say and what we do not

We say we are licensed and insured because the plumbers we send are. We do not publish ratings or reviews on this site, and we do not name individuals or licence numbers here. If you want to confirm a plumber's licence, ask when you call.

Calls to our line may be recorded for quality and training purposes.
